# Sketch editing and remaining gaps

Ordinary dragging uses temporary solver targets. The solver projects them into the freedom left by stored constraints, restores those constraints after each accepted step, and commits all solved geometry together. Targets never become dimensions or change the reported degrees of freedom. This supports connected profiles, multiple selected entities, constrained sliding and fixed-radius motion. A free circle rim resizes its radius; a dimensioned rim moves its center when that motion is allowed.

The nonlinear solver uses rank-aware minimum-norm steps. Redundant equations no longer divide by nearly zero pivots, and free coordinates are not chosen arbitrarily by pivot order. This follows the minimum-norm approach described in [Eigen's complete orthogonal decomposition](https://eigen.tuxfamily.org/dox/classEigen_1_1CompleteOrthogonalDecomposition.html). [FreeCAD's solver interface](https://github.com/FreeCAD/FreeCAD/blob/main/src/Mod/Sketcher/App/planegcs/GCS.h) also distinguishes temporary movement constraints from the real sketch system. Our implementation remains Rust and adds no dependency.

Dimension edits validate a candidate and retain its solved seeds before committing. Failed edits leave the prior sketch intact. Directed angular dimensions distinguish opposite directions rather than accepting an angle displaced by 180 degrees.

[Three-point arc](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Sketch/3_point_arc.htm) uses A and the Onshape order: start, end, then a point on the arc. Center arc uses center, start, end, with an explicit clockwise option. The stored entity is `arc` with values `[center_x, center_y, start_x, start_y, end_x, end_y, direction]`; direction is `1` or `-1`. Point indices are center 0, start 1 and end 2. Radius, diameter, coincidence, concentricity and equal-radius constraints apply to arcs. Arc tangency uses shared endpoints with lines or arcs, and an arc endpoint on a circle rim. Major arcs and construction arcs are supported. Display sampling does not define their topology; profiles and solid operations use bounded analytic circles.

[Tangent arc](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Sketch/arc_tangent.htm) starts at an existing line or arc endpoint, or a circle rim, and ends with another click. Click-drag is also supported. The [Line workflow](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Sketch/line.htm) uses Shift+A to switch while chaining; moving away and back near the last endpoint also switches. Placement returns to Line. Tangency is stored as constraints, so changing radius or adjoining geometry regenerates the same relationship. Circle joins use point-on-circle and parallel native radius lines internally, without extra sketch geometry or fixed coordinates. Major arcs retain the outward source direction; circle starts choose the shorter direction because a circle has no directed endpoint. Collinear or coincident arc endpoints reject the gesture atomically.

[Trim](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Sketch/trim.htm) uses M. Clicking removes the interval between exact intersections; a curve without a bound is deleted. Lines, rectangles, circles and arcs are supported, with construction geometry available as bounds. Rectangles become constrained line segments. Valid surviving endpoint, radial and structural constraints remain; invalidated span dimensions or vanished endpoint references are removed and reported. New endpoints follow the intersecting curves through constraints. Ambiguous overlaps or a failed rebuild reject the whole edit. Dragging across several curves to trim them is not implemented.

The next everyday modeling gap is persistent **Use/project** references to existing model edges. Face attachment and frozen external planes are available, but they do not replace a projected outline or edge that follows upstream topology. This limits holes and clearances located from existing geometry. [Onshape's constraint workflow](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Sketch/working_with_constraints.htm) makes these cross-sketch references explicit.

Midpoint and symmetry constraints are the next small constraint additions. Offset and sketch mirror would reduce repeated dimensioning for walls and symmetric parts. Slot and sketch fillet would simplify profiles already expressible with lines, arcs and tangency. Extend, sketch patterns, polygons and standalone points remain absent. Splines, ellipses, text and imported drawing geometry can follow the core mechanical workflows. These are gaps, not implemented toolbar aliases; see [Onshape's sketch tool inventory](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Sketch/sketch_tools.htm).
